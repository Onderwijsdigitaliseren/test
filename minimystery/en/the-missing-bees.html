The Missing Bees

No buzzing. The hive is empty.

The beehive behind the school is empty: the bees have left. Bees only stay where there are enough flowers, water and places to shelter. So something is wrong in the neighbourhood. At nature stations around (or inside) the school you make real observations: counting flowers, recognising trees, minibeasts under a stone, one minute of listening to birds, water, soil, weather, litter, stone or green. Each station gives a nature key; put all the keys in order and the cycle is complete.

Who is playing

  1. For the leader: choose School or After-school, the age group and the length (6, 8 or 10 stations). Start the case on your own phone: the briefing shows the case code. Every team enters that code and gets exactly the same case. Below the briefing is the leader's sheet: the story, a lesson plan for before and after per age group, all stations with answers, hints and fun facts.
  2. Outdoors with map: the stations are close together (all within about 300 metres). The children see a story map with an arrow and cold/warm/hot. Outdoors without map: the stations come one after another; the team walks to a new spot by itself. In the building: the leader picks spots in the school; the observations then happen at the window, by plants in the classroom or in the playground.
  3. Each station is a real observation: counting flowers, recognising a tree by its leaf, minibeasts under a stone, one minute of quiet listening to birds, looking for water, feeling the soil, measuring the weather without instruments, counting litter, animal tracks, reading the season, shelters, pollination, stone or green in ten steps, who eats whom, and what you can do yourself. The team first types what it counted; the follow-up question calculates with that. So every team gets a different result, and you can compare them in class.
  4. Three levels: ages 6–8 (looking, counting, more/less; two pictures as answers and everything read aloud), ages 8–10 (recognising species, food chain, seasons, measuring) and ages 10–12 (ecosystem, cycles, biodiversity, percentages, cause and effect). One hint per station, and a fun fact after every answer.
  5. Finale: put the nature keys in the order of the cycle: sun → plant → animal → soil. If it's right, the buzzing comes back and the team gets a Nature Detective certificate (A4).

Outdoor rules: put stones back, don't pick anything, let minibeasts live, don't pick up litter with bare hands. Teams stay together and on the pavement. No account, no app: it works in the browser.
