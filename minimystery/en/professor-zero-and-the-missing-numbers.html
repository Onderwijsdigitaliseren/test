Professor Zero and the Missing Numbers

Numbers? Pointless! – Prof. Zero

All the numbers in the school are gone: from the clock, the doors, the calendar and the till. Professor Zero thinks numbers are pointless. You will prove him wrong. At maths stations around (or inside) the school you count, measure, estimate and calculate with real things: steps, windows, house numbers, the clock, the till, the traffic. Each station gives a number key; put all the keys in order and the safe opens.

Who is playing

  1. For the leader: choose School or After-school, the age group and the length (6, 8 or 10 stations). Start the case on your own phone: the briefing shows the case code. Every team enters that code and gets exactly the same case. Below the briefing is the leader's sheet: the story, a lesson plan for before and after per age group, all stations with the calculation, hints and maths tips.
  2. Outdoors with map: the stations are close together (all within about 300 metres). The children see a story map with an arrow and cold/warm/hot. Outdoors without map: the stations come one after another; the team walks to a new spot by itself. In the building: the leader picks spots in the school; each spot gets a card with a code from the leader's sheet.
  3. Each station is a real measurement: counting steps to a post, counting windows, finding a house number, lamp posts, counting traffic for one minute, paving stones, floors, shapes, the clock without numbers, the till, sharing fairly, map and scale. The team first types what it counted itself; the follow-up question calculates with that. So every team has a different answer, and the game still checks it.
  4. Three levels following the Dutch maths curriculum: ages 6–8 (counting to 20 and 100, more/less, odd/even, o'clock, money up to 10, two pictures as answers and everything read aloud), ages 8–10 (times tables, division with remainders, cm and m, quarter hours, money with decimals, rounding) and ages 10–12 (decimals, percentages, ratio and scale, area, large numbers). One hint per station, and a maths tip after every answer.
  5. Finale: the number keys from small to large (ages 10–12 with decimals, fractions and percentages mixed). If it's right, the number safe opens, the numbers come back and the team gets a Maths Detective certificate (A4).

Tip: agree on the play area and a finish time. Teams stay together and on the pavement. No account, no app: it works in the browser.
