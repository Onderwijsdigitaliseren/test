Sinterklaas Eve, 5 December · ages 6 to 10

Operation Sinterklaas Eve

The last clue leads to the presents

Piet Pepernoot has hidden the presents for your house perfectly. So well that even he needs his hiding map. And it has just blown to pieces. The pieces are scattered around the neighbourhood or the house, each behind a Piet trial: counting pepernoten, running over the roofs, Ozosnel's hoofbeat, secret code, catching sweets. All the pieces together lead to the presents you really hid.

  1. For the parents, beforehand: hide the presents and tap Head Piet (behind a 4-digit code; the button gives nothing away). Fill in what's there, a clue (can be a riddle) and the exact place in case they can't find it; a photo of the place is optional. One place for everything, or a place for each child. You can also do this during the game. If you fill in nothing, the map points to the living room at the end: put the presents there.
  2. Indoors at home: there's a piece of the hiding map in every room; the game says which room. Outdoors with map: Piet puts the pieces at places in your neighbourhood; the children see a drawn winter map with an arrow and cold/warm/hot, and the grown-up can look at the real map. Outdoors without map: the pieces come one after another; you walk to a new place yourselves.
  3. Every piece is earned with a Piet trial: counting pepernoten, catching sweets with the shoe, shoe memory, running over the roofs, copying Ozosnel's hoofbeat, sorting presents, the odd present, Piet's secret code, the Rhyme Piet, the Big Book, finding and photographing something real, looking and counting, Piet gym (jumping with the phone in your hand) and guessing shadows. Two levels: Pepernoot (6–7, everything read aloud, choose an answer) and Head Piet (8–10, trickier, sometimes typing). One hint per trial; no hints means three stars.
  4. The finale: all the pieces on the table, turn them over, and Piet reads the map aloud: the parents' clue, one hiding place at a time. "We can't find it" whispers the exact place. Then Sinterklaas appoints the group as Piet Detectives, with a diploma (A4) to print.
  5. Stuck? In the menu (☰) the grown-up can skip a trial, behind the code. With the case code, two phones play the same case.

Tip: one phone per group and a grown-up nearby. Camera, microphone and motion sensor are only used on the device itself; photos and hiding places stay on this phone. Outdoors: stay on the pavement and don't run.
