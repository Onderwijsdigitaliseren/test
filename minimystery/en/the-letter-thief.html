The Letter Thief

The sign on the wall says S_HO_L!

Last night the Letter Thief stole letters: from the sign on the wall, from the classroom doors, from the board in the hall. He left a note: whoever wants to find me must make my sentence. At word stations around (or inside) the school you read, spell and build with real words from the neighbourhood: street signs, rhymes, syllables, compound words, punctuation. Each station gives a word key; put all the words in order and his sentence appears.

Who is playing

  1. For the leader: choose School or After-school, the age group and the length (6, 8 or 10 stations). Start the case on your own phone: the briefing shows the case code. Every team enters that code and gets exactly the same case. Below the briefing is the leader's sheet: the story, a lesson plan for before and after per age group, all stations with answers, hints and language tips.
  2. Outdoors with map: the stations are close together (all within about 300 metres). The children see a story map with an arrow and cold/warm/hot. Outdoors without map: the stations come one after another; the team walks to a new spot by itself. In the building: the leader picks spots in the school; each spot gets a card with a code from the leader's sheet.
  3. Each station uses real words from the neighbourhood: copying the street sign and counting its letters, vowels or syllables, clapping the longest word, rhyming with what you see, gluing words into compound words, finding opposites, who does what (word classes), spelling, the alphabet, punctuation, building a sentence, plurals, figurative language, words from other languages, a note to the thief. At the sign stations the team first types the word; the follow-up question works with it, so every team has a different answer.
  4. Three levels building up step by step: ages 6–8 (sounds and letters, syllables, rhyme, capital letter and full stop; two pictures as answers and everything read aloud), ages 8–10 (spelling rules, word classes, compound words, synonyms and opposites, the alphabet, punctuation) and ages 10–12 (verb spelling, grammar, figurative language, loanwords, register). One hint per station, and a language tip after every answer.
  5. Finale: put the word keys in the right order to make a sentence (capital letter at the start, full stop at the end). Then the Letter Thief appears, brings the letters back and the team gets a Word Detective certificate (A4) with his sentence on it.

Tip: agree on the play area and a finish time. Teams stay together and on the pavement. No account, no app: it works in the browser.
