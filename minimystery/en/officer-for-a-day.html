Officer for a Day

All units. Every street sign has been stolen.

Today you are the police. Follow the leads, solve police puzzles, collect golden digits and work out which of the five suspects has the street signs. Then: the evidence cabinet, the line-up, the handcuffs and a real police diploma. Pick the level and the length; the puzzles adjust.

–0 Day streak0 Caught
How long?

  1. Pick the length: Short (5 places, ± 30 min), Normal (8 places) or Long (11 places, ± 75 min). With map: the chief pins the leads on places nearby. Walk there; a police puzzle is waiting. Without map or indoors: you get the puzzles one after another, without places. Works anywhere.
  2. Pick the level: as a Detective the puzzles are trickier and the evidence cabinet code goes in backwards. The puzzles: fingerprint, composite sketch, footprint, copying the siren, binoculars, a colour code on the radio, a chase, the thief in the crowd, remembering a number plate, sorting evidence bags, an interview, map reading and cracking a combination lock. No reading needed: the phone reads everything aloud (Read aloud button).
  3. After every puzzle you get a street sign back, a lead and a golden digit. In between, the thief and the chief pop up, and sometimes there is a photo mission. Evidence is always true and shows a feature: hat, shirt or shoes. Witnesses help too, but they sometimes get it wrong: only the evidence really counts.
  4. When all leads are in, it turns out the thief has locked the handcuffs in the evidence cabinet. Open it with the golden digits. Then the line-up: five suspects in a row in 3D. Tap the culprit. Handcuffs on, a confession, and the group gets a photo album, a police diploma and a WANTED poster with their own photos.

Tip for the grown-up: read the evidence aloud to the whole group, that is the best moment. For the youngest officers Indoors or Outside without map works best; the map mode is for children who can read a little. With an adult along. One phone per group; with the case code more phones play the same case. The photos stay on the phone. Outside: stay on the pavement.
