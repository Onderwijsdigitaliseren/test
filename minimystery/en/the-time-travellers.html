The Time Travellers

The school clock is a time machine!

The clock in the hall is gone, and an old notebook from 1923 explains why: whoever winds it the wrong way makes a leak in time. There are now time leaks around (or inside) your school. At each leak you look into an era, do an investigation in your own neighbourhood and earn a time key. Put the eras in order and the clock comes back. The ten eras of Dutch history, for ages 8–12 and after-school clubs.

Who is playing

  1. For the leader: choose School or After-school, the age and the length (6, 10 or 12 eras). Start the time journey on your own phone: the briefing shows the case code. Every team enters that code (button Enter a case code) and gets exactly the same time journey. Below the briefing is the leader's sheet: the story, a short lesson plan for before and after, all missions with answers and hints.
  2. Outdoors with map: the time leaks are at spots close together (all within about 300 metres). The children see a story map with an arrow and cold/warm/hot. Outdoors without map: the missions come one after another; the team walks to a new spot by itself. In the building: the leader picks spots in the school (hall, gym, library, stairs…). That choice is stored in the case code. For each spot you print a card with a code from the leader's sheet; the team walks to the spot and types the code. Playing without cards works too.
  3. Each time leak is an era from the Dutch history canon: hunters and farmers, Greeks and Romans, monks and knights, towns and states, explorers and reformers, regents and monarchs, wigs and revolutions, citizens and steam engines, world wars, television and computers. Long adds "your own time" and "the future". First a short time journey (story), then an investigation in the neighbourhood or in the building (looking for traces, counting, comparing), then a question at level 6–7, 8–9 or 10–12 years. One hint per mission.
  4. After each right answer: a time key, a "did you know", and the neighbourhood check: has the team seen a trace from this era? It goes into the logbook and on the certificate, and is the starting point for the class discussion afterwards.
  5. Finale: put the eras in order from old to new (with years shown for ages 6–9). If it's right, time flows again, the clock comes back and the team gets a Time Traveller certificate (A4) with their timeline.

Tip: agree on the play area and a finish time. Teams stay together and on the pavement. No account, no app: it works in the browser.
